The idea is simple: there's a flower on screen with four control points, and you just drag them around. As you do, the whole bloom bends, stretches, and responds in real time — petals folding and opening as you move. It's not a video or a looping clip; it's actual data binding driving the geometry, so every frame reacts to exactly where your finger is.
I implemented the whole thing in Flutter — loading the animation, wiring up the four draggable points, mapping touches to the right spots so the flower moves naturally, and adding little touches like a soft glow, a gradient backdrop, and a gentle fade-in so it feels alive rather than just functional. There are also Bounce Back and Reset controls so you can spring the petals back or start fresh anytime.
The best part is how playful it ends up being — you start dragging just to test it and then keep going because it's weirdly satisfying to watch the shape move.
